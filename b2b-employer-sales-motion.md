# Selling This Product to Employers (the B2B Playbook)

This document is a step-by-step guide to selling the *Service für Alltag und Bürokratie in Deutschland* product to mid-sized German companies who hire workers from abroad. "B2B" just means "business-to-business" — selling to companies instead of to individuals. The opposite is "B2C" (business-to-consumer).

Selling to employers is the fastest way to make real money from this product. A typical mid-sized German employer hiring 20 international workers per year will happily pay €500–2,000 per worker for a packaged solution that makes that worker actually show up and stay. That's €10,000–€40,000 per customer per year, locked in by contract.

Compare that to selling to individuals one at a time at €15/month each. You'd need 60–250 individual subscribers to equal one mid-sized employer. The employer is also less likely to cancel, takes way less effort to support per dollar of revenue, and brings in 20 new users in one signed contract.

This document covers:

- Why selling to employers is the right first move
- Who exactly to target (the "ideal customer profile")
- What to charge
- The sales process from first email to signed contract
- The actual emails to send
- The questions to ask in the first call
- How to handle objections
- How to land the first five customers

## 1. Why B2B-Employer First

A side-by-side comparison of the two paths:

| What we're measuring | Selling to individuals (B2C) | Selling to employers (B2B) |
|---|---|---|
| **CAC** — Customer Acquisition Cost. How much you spend to get one customer. | €30–80 per user (mostly ad spend) | €5,000–€15,000 per company (mostly your time selling) |
| **ACV** — Annual Contract Value. How much revenue one customer is worth in a year. | €60–600 per year | €15,000–€100,000 per year |
| **Sales cycle** — How long from first contact to first payment. | Seconds (they sign up online) | 3–6 months for the first deals |
| **LTV** — Lifetime Value. How long a customer stays and what they pay total. | 12–36 months | 4–7 years |
| **How you find customers** | Google ads, Facebook ads, SEO grind | Direct emails, LinkedIn messages, partnerships |
| **Trust barrier** | Hard — they're cold | Easier — the employer vouches for you |
| **Compliance risk** | The user takes the risk | The employer takes the risk and pays to remove it |

A lot of this is the same math VC-backed startups use to decide which channel to bet on. Translated to plain English:

- B2C looks cheaper per customer, but the customers churn fast and don't pay much. You need millions of them.
- B2B looks much more expensive per customer, but each one pays 50x more and stays 3x longer.

There's also a deeper logic. Imagine you're the head of HR (Human Resources — the team that hires and supports employees) at a German software company that just spent €15,000 on a recruiter to find a senior engineer in Brazil. Then it took 60 days to get the visa, another 30 days for the actual move, plus 30 more days where the engineer is technically employed but can't work effectively because they're stuck in Anmeldung lines and on hold with health insurance providers. That's 120 days of paying a senior salary for very little output. The full cost of one hire is somewhere between €80,000 and €150,000 once you count recruiter fees, the lost productivity, plus the risk that the engineer says "screw it" and goes back home (which actually happens — industry data suggests dropout rates of 10–20% for international hires).

If you can cut even half of that admin-related lost productivity, the HR team saves €5,000–€15,000 per hire just on time. If you can also reduce dropout from 18% to 4% — which is what one realistic case study looks like — you save tens of thousands more per hire that doesn't quit.

So the offer to the employer is: "Pay us €1,000 per hire to save €10,000 of yours."

That's an easy yes.

## 2. Who Exactly to Target (the Ideal Customer Profile)

"Ideal Customer Profile" (often shortened to ICP) is just a description of the kind of customer most likely to buy from you and stay happy. Selling to the wrong companies is the most common founder mistake — you waste months chasing deals that never close.

For this product, the right target looks like:

### Company shape ("firmographics")

- **200–2,000 employees.** Smaller than that and they don't have an HR function or a budget. Bigger than that (like a DAX-40 corporation — the 40 biggest German companies) and their procurement process takes 12–18 months, which kills you before they sign.
- **Hiring 5–50 international workers per year.** Below 5 they don't feel the pain enough; above 50 they'll probably build internally or use Localyze.
- **Headquartered in Germany** or with a major German subsidiary that does its own hiring.
- **Industries (in order of priority):** software/tech, engineering, healthcare, manufacturing, professional services, biotech/pharma.

### Signals they need help ("trigger events")

If you see any of these, the company is probably in market for what you sell:

- LinkedIn job posts that say "visa sponsorship," "relocation support," "Blue Card eligible." That's HR raising their hand.
- A recent hire with a "Talent Acquisition" or "Global Mobility" title in their job — they're staffing up for international hiring.
- Open roles in occupations on the German government's shortage list ("Mangelberufe" — jobs like nursing, IT, engineering where the government deliberately makes it easier to hire foreign workers).
- News about an expansion or funding round — covered in *Manager Magazin*, *Gründerszene*, *Handelsblatt*.
- Already using Localyze, [Lingoda for Business](https://www.lingoda.com/en/business/), [Sirelo](https://www.sirelo.com/), or a big immigration law firm (Fragomen, Newland Chase, EY Law). They've already shown they pay for this category.

### Companies to deliberately skip

- **Under 50 employees.** No HR function, no procurement, no budget. Tempting because they're friendly but they take forever to pay €1,000.
- **Government and public sector.** Procurement takes 12–18 months. Not worth it until you're Series B.
- **DAX-40 (Germany's biggest companies).** Same procurement problem. Revisit when you're bigger.

## 3. Who Actually Decides (the Buying Committee)

In any B2B sale you need to convince multiple people, not just one. The group is called the "buying committee." Knowing who's in it saves you from spending three months selling to someone who doesn't have the power to say yes.

| Role | What they actually do | What they care about |
|---|---|---|
| **Head of People / CHRO** (Chief Human Resources Officer) | The economic buyer — they sign the check | Cost per hire, retention, employer brand |
| **Talent Acquisition Lead** | Runs the team that recruits | Candidate experience, dropout reduction, time from offer to acceptance |
| **Global Mobility Manager** (when one exists) | Owns the relocation process | Compliance, paperwork burden, vendor consolidation |
| **Hiring Manager** | The team lead who wants the new hire | How fast the new hire becomes productive |
| **IT / Security** | Blocker | GDPR, data security, single sign-on, where data is stored |
| **Procurement** | Blocker | Contract terms, payment terms, references |
| **Legal** | Blocker | Data processing agreement, liability, indemnification (who pays if something goes wrong) |

A "blocker" doesn't mean these people are evil. They're people whose job is to say "wait, prove this is safe" before the company signs. You don't sell to them so much as you prepare clean answers to their questions in advance, so they never become a roadblock.

**The champion** — the person inside the company who actually pushes the deal forward — is almost always the Talent Acquisition Lead. They feel candidate dropout pain personally; they're the ones losing offers. Start there.

## 4. Pricing — Three Models to Test

Three pricing models. You'll A/B test them in Year 1 to see which one closes best.

### Model A — Pay per hire

The simplest model. Each time the company hires an international worker, they pay you a one-time fee.

- Standard pack: €499 — EU national hire, basic everyday-life support.
- Plus pack: €999 — Non-EU hire with Skilled Worker visa, family up to 2.
- Pro pack: €1,999 — Blue Card or complicated case, family up to 4, plus degree recognition.
- Crisis pack: €2,999 — visa expiring, deportation risk, urgent escalation.

Easiest to say yes to ("we'll try just one"), hardest to forecast for your business.

### Model B — Platform plus per-seat

Monthly subscription plus per-user fee. This is how SaaS (Software as a Service) products usually price.

- Base platform fee: €500/month
- Per active user: €29–49/month per employee, for their first 12 months
- Volume discounts at 10, 25, 50, 100 users

Predictable revenue but harder to sell to first-time customers ("we don't know if we'll actually use it").

### Model C — Annual subscription, unlimited

One annual price, unlimited usage.

- Tier 1 (up to 10 hires/year): €9,990/year
- Tier 2 (up to 25): €19,990
- Tier 3 (up to 50): €34,990
- Tier 4 (unlimited): €59,990
- Pilot offer: 3 hires free for 90 days

Best long-term economics for both sides, but biggest upfront commitment from the customer.

**Recommendation:** Start with Model A for the first 5 customers. It's the easiest "yes." Then once they're using the product and seeing value, migrate them to Model C at renewal time. The renewal is the natural moment to upsell because they've already proven they need this.

## 5. What You Actually Sell to Employers

The B2C product (the one individuals use) does most of the work. But employers need a few extra features layered on top:

1. **HR admin dashboard.** A single screen showing the status of every relocating employee. "Anna: Anmeldung done. Bank account in progress. Health insurance pending." Saves the HR person from having to ask each new hire how it's going.

2. **Bulk onboarding.** Invite 10 new hires by email at once. Each one self-serves through the product. HR doesn't have to babysit.

3. **Compliance reports.** Proof for the company's auditors that all required documents were collected and all deadlines met. Big companies need this for their own legal reasons.

4. **Branded experience.** The new hire opens the app and sees the employer's logo and a "Welcome to [Company]!" greeting. Tiny detail, huge perceived value.

5. **Single Sign-On (SSO) and SCIM.** Lets the employer add and remove employees through their existing identity tool (Google Workspace, Microsoft Entra/Azure AD, Okta). Required for any company with more than a few hundred employees.

6. **Dedicated customer success manager** at higher tiers. A real human at our company who knows the customer and proactively checks in.

## 6. The Sales Process (the Funnel)

A "funnel" is just the sales journey from first contact to closed deal. It's called a funnel because thousands enter at the top, dozens reach the middle, only a handful close at the bottom.

Here's what each stage looks like and roughly how long it takes:

| Stage | What happens | When you can move on | Typical time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Prospect** | Build a list of companies that fit the ICP | They look like a fit | — |
| **1. Outreach** | Send personalized email + LinkedIn message | They reply or accept a meeting | 1–3 weeks |
| **2. Discovery call** | 30-minute call to learn their situation | You've confirmed real pain and there's budget | 1 week |
| **3. Demo** | 45-minute walkthrough of the product + ROI calculator | A champion is identified inside; security questions raised | 1–2 weeks |
| **4. Security review** | They review your data-protection setup and contracts | IT and legal sign off | 2–6 weeks |
| **5. Pilot** | 3 hires free for 90 days or 1 paid pilot at 50% off | Pilot completes, customer is happy | 90 days |
| **6. Procurement** | Contract negotiation, payment terms, renewal terms | Contract signed | 2–4 weeks |
| **7. Launch** | Kickoff call, first employees onboarded | First hire goes through the product | 2 weeks |

**Total cycle:** 4–6 months for the first few customers. Once you have references and a polished sales process, this drops to 2–3 months.

A few terms in that table that need explaining:

- **Discovery call.** A 30-minute first call where you mostly listen. You're trying to understand if their problem matches your solution. You are not pitching yet.
- **Demo.** A live walkthrough where you show them the product solving their actual problem. Best demos are interactive — they share their real situation and you show how the product handles it.
- **ROI calculator.** ROI = Return On Investment. A spreadsheet that plugs in their numbers (how many hires, what HR costs per hour, etc.) and shows them the savings. Filled out live during the call, it is the single most effective sales tool in this category.
- **Security review.** The IT and security teams want to confirm you store data in the EU, encrypt everything, and don't leak. You give them a one-page security packet plus your DPA (Data Processing Agreement — a standard legal document under GDPR).
- **Pilot.** A free or discounted trial. The first 5 customers get 3 hires free for 90 days, no commitment. The cost to you is small; the value of getting them to actually use the product is huge.
- **Procurement.** The company's internal process for signing contracts. Big companies have entire departments for this. They will ask for specific clauses, references, and probably your insurance certificates.

## 7. The Actual Emails to Send

Stop staring at a blank page. Use these templates and personalize the bracketed bits.

### Cold email v1 — to the Head of Talent Acquisition

> Subject: Cutting 90 days of admin pain for [Company]'s international hires
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Saw [Company] is hiring [specific role] from outside the EU. Most TA leads I talk to say candidate dropout in the visa/relocation gap is their #2 pain after sourcing.
>
> We help mid-sized German employers reduce that dropout. One customer cut their pre-arrival dropout from 18% to 4% in two quarters, and saved their HR team about 30 hours per hire on admin (Anmeldung, health insurance, government letters, etc.) — in 12 languages including [their likely candidate languages, e.g., Arabic and Hindi].
>
> Worth a 20-minute call this week or next?
>
> [Your name] | [Your company]
> [Calendar link]

Why this works:

- The subject line names a specific outcome (90 days of admin pain) and the company.
- The first sentence proves you actually looked at their hiring.
- The second sentence puts a number on the pain ("#2 pain after sourcing") and quotes the person you're emailing.
- The third sentence has a real proof point with specific numbers ("18% to 4%").
- The ask is small (20 minutes), specific (this week or next), and easy (just click the calendar link).

### LinkedIn DM v1 — short, casual

> Saw [Company]'s [role] post mentioning visa sponsorship. We help German employers cut international-hire admin from ~30 hours to ~3 per case. Worth a quick call?

LinkedIn DMs need to be shorter than emails. Same logic, fewer words.

### Cold email v2 — to the CHRO (when the TA lead is already a fan)

> Subject: [Company]'s international hires — pilot data after 90 days
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> [Champion name] and I have been running a no-cost pilot with 3 of [Company]'s recent hires. Results so far: [data points — e.g., "all three completed Anmeldung in under 14 days vs. your previous 28-day average"]. Wanted to share before [Champion name] writes it up for you. 15 minutes this week?

This email only goes out after you've already done a pilot. It works because the CHRO is already aware of the project through their team, but they haven't seen the data yet. You're the one bringing the receipts.

## 8. The 12 Discovery Questions

In the discovery call, you mostly ask, mostly listen. These twelve questions surface 80% of what you need to qualify the deal.

1. How many international hires did you complete in the last 12 months?
2. Of those, how many are non-EU?
3. What percentage of accepted offers actually arrive and start?
4. Walk me through what your TA/HR team does between offer acceptance and Day 1. Whose calendar does it land on?
5. What's the average time from offer-accepted to first-day-productive?
6. Who handles visa paperwork — in-house, immigration lawyer, an agency?
7. What does each visa case cost you all-in?
8. What happens when a hire gets a government letter they don't understand? (This question alone surfaces 80% of the pain.)
9. Have you ever lost a hire because the bureaucracy was too painful?
10. Are you using Localyze or anyone similar today? If yes, where are the gaps?
11. Who decides on tools like this? What's the typical procurement timeline?
12. What would success look like 6 months after we go live?

Question 8 is the magic one. Almost every HR person has a story about a panicked employee, a Finanzamt letter that nobody could read, and a frantic translation effort. That story is the moment they "get" the product.

## 9. The ROI Calculator (the One Slide That Closes Deals)

Numbers close deals. Show this in a spreadsheet during the call, with their actual inputs:

| Input | Default value |
|---|---|
| International hires per year | 20 |
| HR hours per hire on admin | 25 |
| Loaded HR hourly cost (salary + benefits ÷ hours) | €70 |
| **HR admin cost per year** | **€35,000** |
| Candidate dropout rate before arrival | 15% |
| Cost per failed hire (re-recruit) | €30,000 |
| **Annual cost of dropouts** | **€90,000** |
| **Total annual cost of current process** | **€125,000** |
| | |
| Our annual platform fee | €25,000 |
| Expected dropout reduction with our product | 60% |
| Expected HR time reduction | 80% |
| **Estimated annual savings** | **€82,000** |
| **Net return on investment** | **3.3× / €57,000 saved** |

Adjust the defaults live in the meeting based on their answers from discovery. The point is to anchor the conversation on their money, not yours.

## 10. Handling Objections

Every B2B sale hits the same objections. Memorize the responses.

| What they say | What to say back |
|---|---|
| **"We already use Localyze (or Sirelo, or competitor)."** | "Great. Where do they fall short? Most customers tell us they're strong on relocation logistics but weak on (a) ongoing everyday-life support after arrival, (b) multilingual letter handling, (c) the employee experience. We complement them or replace them, depending on your needs. Happy to do a side-by-side." |
| **"We use an immigration lawyer."** | "We complement them. They cost €3,000–€5,000 per case and handle the legal core. We handle the 90% of admin that doesn't need a lawyer — Anmeldung, health insurance, banking, letters, everyday life — at a fraction of the cost. Many of our customers pair us with their existing law firm." |
| **"Our HR can do this."** | "They can. Our customers' HR teams could too. The question is whether it's worth about 30 hours per hire of HR time, especially when most of the work is repetitive and language-specific. Want to see the ROI math?" |
| **"GDPR / data security?"** | "EU-only hosting. Encryption at rest. SOC 2 Type 2 [in progress or completed]. ISO 27001 on the roadmap. We can send our DPA and security packet today." |
| **"We don't have budget this year."** | "Understood. The per-hire pack is operating expense, no annual commitment. You can start with one case to see fit. Most customers expand from there." |
| **"Wait, you're not licensed?"** | "Correct, and that's intentional. We handle information, translation, and process navigation — the parts that don't require a license. For actual legal or tax advice, we route to vetted partners. This actually reduces your compliance risk versus an HR employee giving informal advice." |
| **"Can you handle [specific edge case]?"** | If yes: explain how. If no: "Not yet. We can build that for €X by Y if it's a deal-breaker — but our experience is that the long tail is best handled by referring to our partner network." |
| **"Send me a proposal and I'll review."** | "Happy to. Before I send something generic, can we do a 15-minute discovery so I tailor it? Otherwise it goes to the bottom of your inbox." |

The general pattern: never argue. Acknowledge, then redirect with a concrete number or fact.

## 11. Landing the First 5 Customers

The goal for the first 6 months: 5 paying customers ("logos" in sales-speak — because each one looks good as a logo on your investor pitch deck), all founder-led, all in Berlin or Munich.

Why founder-led? Because the founder learns the buyer's language by talking to them directly. Hiring a salesperson before you've sold ~10 deals yourself is a classic mistake. You'd be teaching them to sell a product whose pitch you haven't refined yet.

Month-by-month plan:

| Month | Target | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 50 prospects identified | LinkedIn Sales Navigator + manual research; build the list |
| 1–2 | 20 cold outreaches per week | Email + LinkedIn DM, 2 follow-ups each |
| 2 | 10 discovery calls | Founder takes every single call personally |
| 3 | 5 product demos | Live, screen-share, ROI calculator filled in during the meeting |
| 3–4 | 3 pilots running | 90 days free, 3 hires per pilot |
| 4–6 | 5 signed customers | Convert pilots + 2 net-new |

### The pilot offer (the deal-closer)

The trade you offer the first 5 customers:

- **What they get:** 3 of their next international hires use the product completely free for 90 days. Founder gives them their personal mobile number, available from 9 AM to 9 PM.
- **What you get:** A 30-minute recorded testimonial after the pilot, intros to 3 peer companies, permission to use their logo on your website, structured feedback they help shape during the pilot.

This is unfair to you in dollar terms but extremely valuable in trust terms. The first 5 customers are the foundation of every future sale.

### Where to find the first 50 prospects

Concrete sources:

- LinkedIn search: filter for "Talent Acquisition" titles at companies in Germany with 200–2,000 employees.
- Job boards: filter for "Blue Card," "visa sponsorship," "relocation support."
- *Made in Germany* exporter directories — companies that already hire internationally.
- Berlin and Munich startup lists: *Gründerszene 100*, *Deutsche Startups Top 50*.
- Conferences: *HR Innovation Day*, *Personalmesse*, *Recruiting Convention*, *Web Summit* Berlin.
- LinkedIn communities and Slack groups: *People Ops Germany*, *Talent Acquisition Slack DACH* ("DACH" = Germany/Austria/Switzerland).

## 12. Partnerships That Multiply Reach

Building a sales pipeline from scratch takes forever. Partnerships shortcut it. The right partner has lots of your future customers already.

1. **Employer-of-Record (EOR) platforms.** [Deel](https://www.deel.com), [Remote](https://remote.com), [Multiplier](https://www.usemultiplier.com), [Lano](https://www.lano.io). They handle international payroll and employment but don't do bureaucracy concierge. Cross-referrals make sense.
2. **Immigration law firms.** Fragomen, Newland Chase, EY Law. They want the high-margin legal work; you take the admin off their plate. Mutual referrals.
3. **Relocation companies.** [Sirelo](https://www.sirelo.com), Crown, Santa Fe. They handle the moving truck; you handle the paperwork. Bundle.
4. **Coding bootcamps with placement.** [Le Wagon](https://www.lewagon.com), [Neue Fische](https://www.neuefische.de), [Spiced Academy](https://www.spiced-academy.com), [CODE University](https://code.berlin). Their international students all need this.
5. **Insurance brokers for expats.** [Feather](https://feather-insurance.com), [Ottonova](https://www.ottonova.de). Affiliate plus referral.
6. **Banks specializing in expat onboarding.** [N26](https://n26.com), Bunq, C24. Same deal.
7. **Government partners.** [Goethe-Institut](https://www.goethe.de) and [DAAD](https://www.daad.de) — official channels with pre-arrival reach.
8. **HR consultancies and PEOs** (Professional Employer Organizations — companies that bundle HR services for smaller businesses).

Approach each partner with a one-page brief: "You get X, we get Y, both branded co-marketing."

## 13. Building the Sales Team Over Time

Don't hire too fast. Founder sells first. Then layer in support.

- **Months 0–6:** Founder sells, personally. No other salespeople.
- **Months 6–12:** Hire one SDR (Sales Development Representative — they do outbound prospecting, book meetings, hand off to founder for the close). Founder still closes.
- **Months 12–18:** Hire one AE (Account Executive — closes deals) plus one more SDR. Founder coaches both.
- **Months 18–24:** Three AEs, two SDRs, one sales engineer (technical person who handles security questions in demos). Quarterly business reviews with each customer.

The first AE you hire should look like:

- 3–5 years of SaaS sales experience in DACH
- Native German + English, third language a plus
- Previous experience selling HR tech (e.g., [Personio](https://www.personio.com), [HiBob](https://www.hibob.com), [Sage](https://www.sage.com)) or expat services
- €60,000 base salary plus 50% variable on quota (so €90,000 if they hit target)

## 14. The Numbers to Watch

Pick a small number of metrics and obsess over them. Don't drown in a 50-line dashboard.

| Metric | What it means | Target Year 1 | Target Year 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Logos** | Paying customers | 5 | 25 |
| **ARR** (Annual Recurring Revenue — yearly subscription revenue) | Predictable yearly income | €100,000 | €750,000 |
| **Avg ACV** (Annual Contract Value) | Average customer pays you per year | €20,000 | €30,000 |
| **CAC** (Customer Acquisition Cost) | Cost to land one customer | €15,000 | €10,000 |
| **CAC payback** | Months for one customer's revenue to repay what you spent acquiring them | 9 months | 6 months |
| **Gross retention** | % of last year's customers still here this year (before any upsells) | 85% | 90% |
| **Net retention** | % of last year's revenue this year (including upsells) | 100% | 115% |
| **Pipeline coverage** | Total open deal value ÷ quarter's revenue target. 4× means you have 4x more in your pipeline than you need to close. | 4× | 3× |

These are the same metrics every B2B SaaS company tracks. If you understand them, you can read any tech company's earnings report.

## 15. What to Build Before the First Pitch

Before you start emailing prospects, build these materials. They are the minimum.

- 10-slide sales deck (problem, solution, proof, pricing, next step).
- One-page printable "leave-behind" — the summary they keep after the meeting.
- ROI calculator as a Google Sheet first; turn it into a web tool by Month 3.
- Demo environment with realistic anonymized data so demos don't show your real customers.
- Security one-pager (data residency, encryption, certifications, DPA).
- Standard MSA (Master Service Agreement) and DPA templates, reviewed by a lawyer.
- 3 case-study placeholders — fill them in as pilots complete.
- Pricing one-pager.
- Objection-handling crib sheet (internal use only).
- Outreach email sequences in your CRM (Customer Relationship Management tool — start with [HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com) or [Pipedrive](https://www.pipedrive.com)).

You can build all of this in two weeks if you're disciplined.

## 16. The Three Things That Will Decide Whether This Works

If you do nothing else right, do these three things:

1. **Founder sells the first 10 deals personally.** Not an SDR, not a consultant, not "we should hire a head of sales." The founder closes those deals personally, learns the exact language buyers use, and then hires once the pitch is sharp.

2. **Focus on the right industry.** Tech and engineering have the highest concentration of non-EU hires + the budget + the speed to actually decide. Skip traditional manufacturing and public sector in Year 1.

3. **Anchor every demo on the Behörden-Brief Decoder.** Show a real (anonymized) German letter being decoded live, in the candidate's language, in 5 seconds. That moment is the one that closes calls. Nothing else in the product is as immediately, viscerally impressive.
